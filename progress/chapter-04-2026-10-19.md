## Exact box-level latency without capture (`probe.sh latency`)

- `xdpstamp` stamps arrival at the NIC driver (XDP metadata); kfan built
  with `LAT_HIST=2` adds, per shred, arrival → each copy handed to the NIC
  into in-kernel histograms (1 µs buckets to 1 ms, then 1 ms to ~17 ms):
  first copy, last copy (completion), per destination. `kfan.sh latency` /
  `probe.sh latency SECONDS` read them over a window (no tcpdump, includes
  the queueing before the fan-out) and append to `box-results.jsonl`
  (`source: kfan-hist`, same summary fields, so `fanout-measure compare`
  works).
- Cost: one timestamp + one atomic add per copy (not yet measured on the
  server).

## Latency split: waiting vs sending

- New histogram series in kfan (`LAT_HIST`): arrival at the NIC driver →
  the fan-out program starting on the shred (packet buffer, bond, and any
  queueing behind earlier packets on that CPU). `probe.sh latency` prints it
  under "where the time goes" with its share of the mean completion time; the
  rest is the fan-out sending its copies. Tested end to end (namespaces).

## Exact latency, production (probe.sh latency, 1 × 60 s, no capture)

| Arrival at NIC driver → | p50 | p90 | p99 | p99.9 | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| last copy (completion) | **56.5 µs** | 139.5 | **208.5** | 266.5 | 425.5 |
| first copy | 32.5 | 114.5 | 184.5 | 235.5 | 377.5 |
| waiting before the fan-out starts | 30.5 | 112.5 | 181.5 | 232.5 | 374.5 |

- **Established:** waiting is 64% of the mean completion time; sending all
  15 copies takes ~26 µs (~1.7 µs per copy, per-destination spacing). The
  tail is queueing on the receiving CPU (behind earlier shreds and ~4
  duplicates per shred), not the copies. Resolves the two biased numbers
  (receiver 56/98 missed the waiting; capture probe 83/248 was slowed).
- Next: cut the waiting (drop duplicates in XDP; then spread bursts over
  CPUs), not the per-copy cost.

## Incident: copies refused while loading xdpstamp

- Loading XDP resets the port's queues for ~1 s without the bond noticing;
  the fan-out (direct to ports) had 12,338 copies refused (~0.15% of that
  minute) for destinations on the port being reset.
- **Fix:** `xdpstamp.sh` now moves the fan-out off each port first (kfan.sh
  exclude file, honoured by the service's 1 s port check) and back after the
  link returns; the file is always removed on exit. Tested on a bond.

## Duplicates dropped at the NIC driver (xdpstamp DEDUP=1)

- xdpstamp now optionally drops later copies of a shred in XDP (same 88-byte
  key as kfan), before the kernel builds packet buffers for them, and marks
  first copies "checked"; kfan then skips its own check. Without the mark
  (XDP not loaded, e.g. after a reboot) kfan deduplicates as before.
- Loaded once with bpftool (maps pinned under /sys/fs/bpf/xdpstamp) and
  attached to both bond ports, so copies arriving on different ports are
  caught; replaces a previously loaded stamp program.
- Tested on a bond with two ports, every shred arriving once per port:
  without XDP kfan drops 3000/3000 duplicates; with XDP dedup XDP drops all
  3000, kfan sees 3000 first copies marked checked, both destinations get
  3000/3000 intact. Found and fixed: bpffs rejects "." in names.
- Expected effect (hypothesis): less waiting before the fan-out (p50
  ~31 µs, p99 ~182 µs), since ~4 of 5 packets no longer reach the stack.

## XDP dedup live (14:31 UTC); second refusal incident during the swap

- From 14:32: 4.0 duplicates per shred dropped at the driver, 100% of first
  copies marked checked, kfan's own duplicate drops 0, 15.0 copies per
  shred, refused 0.
- During the swap 20,836 copies were refused although the fan-out moved off
  each port first (journal: moved within 1 s, back after 5 s). dmesg shows no
  driver reset: replacing an attached XDP program is in place on i40e.
  **Hypothesis:** with all 15 copies on one port for ~5 s, bursts exceed that
  port's line rate and its transmit queue overflows (to check: port speed and
  qdisc drop counters). If so: no port move when replacing a program; a
  different approach for first load / unload.

## XDP dedup made the waiting longer (2 × 60 s each, probe.sh latency)

| | before XDP dedup | with XDP dedup |
|---|---|---|
| waiting p50 / p99 | 30.5–32.5 / 181–184 µs | **53.5–54.5 / 209–211 µs** |
| completion p50 / p99 | 56.5–59.5 / 208–212 µs | **77.5–79.5 / 239–241 µs** |
| fan-out kernel CPU | 18.8% of a core | 16.0% |

- **Established:** worse by ~22 µs at p50 (at ~10% lower load); sending time
  unchanged (~24–26 µs). Expected the opposite.
- **Hypothesis:** received packets reach tc in lists of `gro_normal_batch`
  (8) or at the end of the driver poll. With duplicates gone, lists fill ~5×
  slower, so first copies wait for the rest of the poll (incl. all XDP
  duplicate checks). Test: `net.core.gro_normal_batch=1`.
- Port speed 10 Gb/s: all 15 copies on one port overload it in bursts,
  consistent with the swap refusals (qdisc drop counters are cumulative since
  boot, ~600M per port, so not conclusive).

## gro_normal_batch=1, and a correction to the "exact" measurement

- With `net.core.gro_normal_batch=1` (2 × 60 s, XDP dedup on): completion
  p50 25.5, p99 42.5, p99.9 ~90, max ~190 µs; waiting p50 1.5, p99 4.5 µs.
- **Not a true end-to-end result.** The XDP stamp is taken when the driver
  picks a packet up, not when it reached the NIC. One CPU needs ~26 µs per
  shred (15 copies) and bursts arrive faster than that, so shreds must queue
  somewhere: with batching after the stamp (visible), without it in the NIC
  receive ring before the stamp (invisible). The XDP-dedup "regression" is
  most likely the same shift in reverse. **Hypothesis:** the real gain of
  batch=1 is a few µs (no wait for 7 more packets); burst tails unchanged.
  The capture probe has the same blind spot (it also timestamps on pickup).
- Next: a true arrival time (NIC hardware timestamps if the NIC can stamp
  all packets; otherwise an upper bound from when each CPU became busy).
  Batch reverted to 8 meanwhile (system-wide, not persistent).

## True arrival: NIC cannot timestamp all packets; latency now reported as a range

- `ethtool -T`: hardware receive filters are PTP-only (no `all`), so no
  hardware arrival time for shreds.
- `probe.sh latency` now reports low..high for completion, first copy and
  waiting. Low: from driver pickup (misses ring wait). High: from when that
  CPU last went from idle to busy with shreds (a pickup more than 10 µs after
  its previous fan-out ended counts as idle); a shred picked up during a busy
  stretch waited at most since its start. True value in between (high is off
  by at most the interrupt latency on the other side).
- Lab check (WSL veth, 15 destinations, 4 parallel senders): quiet shreds
  low = high (p50 10.5..11.5 µs); overloaded bursts diverge (p99
  384..2524 µs). The high end is pessimistic when a CPU stays busy long.

## gro_normal_batch 8 vs 1, measured as ranges (alternating 8,1,8,1 × 60 s)

| completion low..high | p50 | p90 | p99 |
|---|---|---|---|
| batch 8 (2 runs) | 78.5–81.5 .. 143.5–158.5 µs | 183.5–186.5 .. 591.5–637.5 | 249.5 .. ≥1 ms |
| batch 1 (2 runs) | 25.5 .. 210.5–221.5 µs | 30.5–31.5 .. 708.5–720.5 | 42.5–43.5 .. ≥1 ms |

- **Established:** batch 1 only shifts the clock (low end 78 → 25 µs, high
  end worse: p50 ~150 → ~215, p90 ~615 → ~715 µs). Kept at the default 8.
- **Established:** the receiving CPU is sometimes busy without a break for
  over 1 ms; true completion p99 lies between ~250 µs and >1 ms (high end
  coarse: 1 ms buckets above 1 ms). Shreds of a burst queue behind each
  other's fan-out (~26 µs each); only parallel fan-out (spread a burst over
  CPUs) or cheaper copies (IOMMU passthrough) can shorten that. A change
  counts only if both ends of the range drop.
