# Chapter 2 — 2026-10-15

**Day summary:** switched candidate testing from the lab to live production
traffic, using alternating A/B runs; started our own forwarder,
`shred-fanout`, on branch `fanout-opt`.

## Test on production instead of the lab

- **Why:** the lab baseline is ~5× slower than production, so it could rank
  candidates wrongly; production is the ground truth.
- **How:** `ab.sh` takes over one instance (23998), stops its systemd
  service, alternates the baseline and candidate binaries with the same
  arguments (10 s warmup + 10 s measurement each), and restarts the service
  at the end. Alternating cancels out traffic drift between runs.
- **Decision:** validate with an A/A run (baseline vs itself) first, then
  test `opt_1_1`. The lab stays for basic correctness checks of new
  prototypes before they touch live traffic.

## New forwarder: shred-fanout (branch `fanout-opt`)

- **Why:** a fresh design aimed at the measured bottlenecks, instead of the
  older `opt_1_1` experiment.
- **Design:** one receive thread busy-polls the socket (no wakeup per
  packet) and drops duplicates exactly; each unique shred goes once into a
  lock-free broadcast ring; 4 pinned sender threads each own a share of the
  destinations and send a shred to all of them in one `sendmmsg`. No locks or
  sleeping hand-offs on the path. Same command line as the proxy's
  `forward-only`, so it drops into `ab.sh`.
- **Cost:** 5 threads spinning at 100% CPU (receiver + 4 senders).
- **Status:** unit-tested (ring, dedup); not yet run on Linux.
- **Next:** A/B against the current proxy on 23998.

## First A/B: shred-fanout v1 is slower

- **Result (6 vs 6 alternating runs on 23998):** completion p50 439 vs
  195 µs and first destination p50 155 vs 87 µs, both **REGRESSED**;
  p99 median 7.96 vs 1.96 ms. Delivery 100%, no duplicates.
- **Hypothesis:** pinning spinning threads does not reserve the cores. Other
  instances' threads and kernel network work still get scheduled onto
  cores 12–16 and share them with spinners that never yield, so our threads
  sit switched out for milliseconds at a time.
- **Decision:** measure each thread's time waiting for a CPU (added to the
  probe) before changing the design.

## Profiling both forwarders: preemption ruled out

- **Result (1 profiled pair):** `shred-fanout` gets packets off the socket
  ~6× sooner (3.7 vs 21 µs) and hands them to senders ~6× sooner (19 vs
  117 µs). Its threads were almost never preempted (16 ms in 10 s) and
  barely waited for a CPU (17 vs 613 ms for the proxy). Yet completion p50
  was still 2× worse (535 vs 271 µs), and each `sendmmsg` call took 56 vs
  15 µs.
- **Conclusion:** the "switched out" hypothesis is rejected. About 100 µs
  per shred is lost in or around the send, after a sender picks up the
  shred.
- **Next:** check for queueing in `bond0`/VLAN devices and whether the
  slowdown follows particular senders or destinations.
