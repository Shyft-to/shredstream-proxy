## Test on production instead of the lab

- **Why:** the lab baseline is ~5× slower than production, so it could rank
  candidates wrongly; production is the ground truth.
- **How:** `ab.sh` takes over one instance (23998), stops its systemd
  service, alternates the baseline and candidate binaries with the same
  arguments (10 s warmup + 10 s measurement each), and restarts the service
  at the end. Alternating cancels out traffic drift between runs.
- **Decision:** validate with an A/A run (baseline vs itself) first, then
  test `opt_1_1`. The lab stays for basic correctness checks of new
  prototypes before they touch live traffic.

## New forwarder: shred-fanout (branch `fanout-opt`)

- **Why:** a fresh design aimed at the measured bottlenecks, instead of the
  older `opt_1_1` experiment.
- **Design:** one receive thread busy-polls the socket (no wakeup per
  packet) and drops duplicates exactly; each unique shred goes once into a
  lock-free broadcast ring; 4 pinned sender threads each own a share of the
  destinations and send a shred to all of them in one `sendmmsg`. No locks or
  sleeping hand-offs on the path. Same command line as the proxy's
  `forward-only`, so it drops into `ab.sh`.
- **Cost:** 5 threads spinning at 100% CPU (receiver + 4 senders).
- **Status:** unit-tested (ring, dedup); not yet run on Linux.
- **Next:** A/B against the current proxy on 23998.
