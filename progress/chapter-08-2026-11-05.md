## Senders: is the 50 µs spin running, and is it enough?

- Confirmed live on the tuned proxy: process environment has
  `SEND_SPIN_US=50`; a binary without the spin would ignore the variable
  silently, so the behaviour was checked too.
- Over the proxy's ~16 h: 609,144,686 voluntary sleeps across the 23
  senders = **~460 sleeps per sender per second**. Each sleep follows a
  50 µs spin (~2.3% CPU) plus a few µs of sending per batch: matches the
  measured 3.4–5.4% CPU per sender (10 s `top` averages).
- **Reading:** most batches still pay a wake-up (~10–15 µs): at ~4k
  shreds/s the next batch is usually more than 50 µs away. The 0 → 50 µs
  step already gave p50 −24% (chapter 7), so a longer window may give more.
- CPU bound per sender: sleeps/s × spin. 200 µs ≤ ~9% (~2 cores for 23),
  1 ms ≤ ~46% (~10 cores, would crowd the listener and blockrazor's proxy;
  crowded cores overflowed the listen socket in chapter 7). 200 µs chosen.

## Fra CPU: no deep sleep states, and no boost

- Question (operator): can every CPU be kept out of C2 and deeper?
- **Already the case (almost certainly):** AMD EPYC 4564P (16 cores,
  32 threads), kernel 6.8, cpuidle driver `none`: the firmware offers
  Linux no idle states, nothing on the kernel command line, nothing in
  dmesg. Idle CPUs only halt (C1, ~1 µs exit). turbostat cannot show
  residency without a cpuidle driver, so the BIOS setting (Global
  C-State Control) is the only direct proof.
- **But the cores never boost:** `amd-pstate-epp` active, EPP
  `performance`, Linux limits 545 MHz–5.88 GHz (`scaling_max_freq`
  5,881,000), boost supported and active; yet every CPU runs at
  4.44–4.49 GHz when busy (turbostat, 5 s), CPU 14 at 99.99% busy
  included: nominal 4.50 GHz, never above.
- Likely cause (hypothesis): C-states disabled in the BIOS. On Zen, boost
  above nominal draws its headroom from idle cores being power gated
  (CC6); with every idle core held in C1 the firmware stays at nominal.
  Ruling out power/temperature first: `turbostat --show PkgWatt,PkgTmp`.
- Option (BIOS + reboot, operator's call): enable C-states, then keep the
  hot CPUs (listener, dispatch, and their siblings) out of deep states from
  Linux per CPU (`cpuidle/state*/disable`), letting the other cores sleep
  to give the busy ones up to 5.88 GHz (+~30% clock). Gain at the box:
  a few µs per shred: invisible to geyserbench (rerun spread ~0.2 ms,
  gap to Triton ~1.5 ms). Low priority.
