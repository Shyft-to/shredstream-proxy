## Senders: is the 50 µs spin running, and is it enough?

- Confirmed live on the tuned proxy: process environment has
  `SEND_SPIN_US=50`; a binary without the spin would ignore the variable
  silently, so the behaviour was checked too.
- Over the proxy's ~16 h: 609,144,686 voluntary sleeps across the 23
  senders = **~460 sleeps per sender per second**. Each sleep follows a
  50 µs spin (~2.3% CPU) plus a few µs of sending per batch: matches the
  measured 3.4–5.4% CPU per sender (10 s `top` averages).
- **Reading:** most batches still pay a wake-up (~10–15 µs): at ~4k
  shreds/s the next batch is usually more than 50 µs away. The 0 → 50 µs
  step already gave p50 −24% (chapter 7), so a longer window may give more.
- CPU bound per sender: sleeps/s × spin. 200 µs ≤ ~9% (~2 cores for 23),
  1 ms ≤ ~46% (~10 cores, would crowd the listener and blockrazor's proxy;
  crowded cores overflowed the listen socket in chapter 7). 200 µs chosen.

## Senders spin 200 µs (`tuned-spin200`)

- `SEND_SPIN_US=200` on the tuned proxy; restart covered by the standby
  ("no forwarder" 0; "to standby" is cumulative since attach, 28,112).
- 10 s after settling: **92 sleeps per sender per second (vs ~460, −80%)**,
  **6.4% CPU per sender** (vs 3.4–5.4%): ~+0.5 core in all. Most batches
  now arrive while their sender is still spinning.
- Caveat: the ~460 is a 16 h average, not a matching 10 s window; if the
  result is borderline, re-measure at 50 µs the same way.
- Expected (hypothesis): p50 and first-copy latency a little lower; p99
  unchanged (set by the 10 Gb/s wire, chapter 7).
- **Geyserbench (operator): no improvement** over the 50 µs config
  (numbers to be added). Reverted to `SEND_SPIN_US=50`; geyserbench re-run
  on `kdedup-to23998-sb23999_tuned_df-off` as the reference. Fewer
  wake-ups (~10–15 µs each, at the p50) do not show at the node, whose
  latency is set by FEC-set completion and the wire.
