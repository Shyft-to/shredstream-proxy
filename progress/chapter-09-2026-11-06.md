## FEC-set readiness at .76 (`fec-ready.py`)

- **Why:** the node can rebuild a Merkle FEC set from any k of its
  shreds (k = data shreds, from a coding shred's header), so its latency
  follows set readiness, not per-shred completion across 23 destinations.
- **Metric**, per FEC set, from its first shred reaching the box:
  `arrived` = the box holds k of its shreds (sources' spread; no
  forwarder beats it); `ready` = k of them have left for .76; `box adds`
  = ready − arrived; `all data` = its last data shred has left for .76
  (the old, stricter target). Egress stamp = handed to the NIC driver.
- Script checked on a synthetic capture with known answers (one data
  shred held 500 µs: `all data` 503 µs, `ready` 14 µs).
- **Fra, 2026-11-05 19:15, `kdedup-to23998-sb23999_tuned_df-off`,
  1 × 60 s:** 315,485 shreds, 4,934 sets, 4,846 measured, 0 skipped
  (every set had a coding shred, every set reached k at .76); box probe
  p99 1,131 µs, delivery 100%, ~5.3k shreds/s.

| µs per FEC set | p50 | p90 | p99 | p99.9 | max |

| arrived (box holds k) | 1,067 | 5,578 | 8,951 | 13,192 | 17,531 |

- **Established:**
  - The sets' own arrival spread dominates: 1.1 ms at p50, 9 ms at p99,
    from first shred to the k-th, before any forwarding.
  - The box adds 29 µs per set at p50. Its tail (0.9 ms at p99) falls on
    sets that arrive fast, in bursts (the 10 Gb/s wire, chapter 7): at
    the node's p99 `ready` is only ~10 µs behind `arrived`, at p50
    ~100 µs.
  - Any k of n vs all data shreds: ~90 µs earlier at p50, ~215 µs at
    p99.
- **Reading:** a forwarder that took no time at all would make .76's
  sets ready ≤ ~100 µs sooner at p50 and ~10 µs at p99: below
  geyserbench's rerun spread (~0.2 ms) and far below the ~1.5 ms to
  Triton. Further forwarding work cannot move geyserbench; the time is in
  the sets' arrival (sources, leader pacing, turbine) and after the box
  (node, richat).
- Data first: no on-capture this round, so no on/off comparison. With the
  box's whole p50 share at ~29 µs, data first could not plausibly cost
  0.4–0.6 ms at the node (hypothesis): yesterday's geyserbench regression
  was most likely noise.
