# Chapter 3 — 2026-10-16

**Day summary:** measured on a real receiver for the first time. The kernel
fan-out is fast end to end; AF_XDP is fast up to its send ring but slow from
there to the wire, so its own measurement was misleading.

## AF_XDP baseline, own measurement (8 × 10 s)

Forwarder on core 34, local-send thread on 33, queue interrupts on 35:
all copies p50 ~10 µs, p99 ~69 µs, p99.9 ~95 µs, max 124–232 µs; ~5.4k
shreds/s. Same as 2026-10-15.

## Receiver measurement (new)

- **Method:** one extra destination (10.182.192.17:9910, listed last) gets
  copies whose first 16 payload bytes are a mode tag + the shred's arrival
  time on the forwarder (`kfan` STAMP_PORT, `xskfwd --stamp-port`).
  `stamp-recv` on the receiver subtracts it from the kernel receive time; the
  forwarder's clock is mapped onto the receiver's with NTP-style probes to
  `clock-echo` (fitted every 0.5 s, drift followed). Absolute error ≤ ±16 µs
  (half the fastest probe round trip); the same for both designs, so the
  comparison is unaffected. Tools: `fanout-bench` (`clock-echo`,
  `stamp-recv`, `scripts/build-receiver.sh`).
- **Result** (alternating, 2 rounds × ~2 min each; forwarder arrival → receiver):

| | p50 | p90 | p99 | p99.9 | max |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kernel fan-out, direct to ports (26 windows) | **~53 µs** | ~83 µs | **~93 µs** | ~135 µs | 190–590 µs |
| AF_XDP zero-copy (16 windows) | ~137 µs | ~290 µs | ~1.1 ms | ~1.5 ms | up to 2.3 ms |

- **Established:** AF_XDP hands copies to its send ring in ~10 µs (p50), but
  they reach the receiver ~85 µs later than kernel fan-out's at p50 and
  ~1 ms later at p99. The delay is between the AF_XDP send ring and the wire.
  Kernel fan-out is very stable (p99 91–94 µs in every window).
- **Hypothesis:** the NIC's transmit interrupt moderation for queue 47
  (i40e default tx-usecs ~122 µs) delays AF_XDP sends, which the driver only
  processes in its interrupt-driven poll; the ms tails would be sends left for
  a later poll. Next: per-queue tx-usecs 0 on queue 47; if not enough, let the
  forwarder run the queue's poll itself (busy-poll sockets).
- **Hypothesis:** the box-level probe overstated kernel fan-out (p99 ~270 µs
  then vs ~93 µs to the receiver now), because tcpdump's send-side capture
  runs on the sending CPU for every copy.

## AF_XDP: transmit interrupt delay ruled out

- Queue 47 had tx-usecs 50 (not ~122). Setting it to 0 (both ports) changed
  nothing: receiver p50 ~133 µs, p99 0.36–1.0 ms. **Ruled out.**
- Added to xskfwd: per copy, arrival → the NIC reporting it sent (completion
  ring), to split the gap into NIC/driver vs beyond; and `--busy-poll USECS`
  (`BUSY_POLL=` in xsk.sh): the forwarder runs the queue's driver poll inside
  its own send/receive calls instead of waiting for the interrupt.

## AF_XDP: the delay is in the NIC/driver; busy polling only halves it

- **Established (no busy polling):** copies reach the send ring at p50
  ~10 µs, but the NIC reports them sent at p50 ~116 µs (p99 0.4–1.0 ms); the
  receiver sees them ~15 µs later. The wait is on the forwarder, before the
  driver sends (i40e sends AF_XDP frames only from the queue's poll).
- **Busy polling (`BUSY_POLL=50`, 3 windows / receiver 5 windows):** NIC
  "sent" p50 17–25 µs, but p99 still 0.2–0.8 ms; receiver p50 66–91 µs,
  p99 0.25–1.2 ms. Cost per shred 2.3 → 5.2 µs. Still worse than kernel
  fan-out at every percentile (control in the same session: p50 ~53,
  p99 ~93 µs, unchanged).
- **Open question:** with busy polling, ~50 µs remain between the NIC
  reporting a copy sent and the receiver getting it (was ~15 µs); possibly
  the NIC fetches the frame (completion) well before it transmits it.
- **Recommendation:** park AF_XDP; the kernel fan-out is the better design
  on this NIC/driver and already near the target end to end.

## Deployed service, box-level probe (1 × 60 s, 15 destinations, no probe copy)

- Completion p50 82 µs, p99 242 µs, p99.9 299 µs, max 547 µs; first
  destination p50 57 µs. Delivery 100.000% (371,712 shreds), 0 duplicate
  sends. Comparable to the 2026-10-15 probe of kernel fan-out direct
  (p50 86, p99 ~270 µs).
- Copies leave in table order, ~1.9 µs apart (127.0.0.1 first at p50 56.6,
  10.182.192.66 last at 82.2). With no recent arrivals the first copy
  leaves at p50 ~3 µs; from ~6 arrivals per 500 µs onward shreds queue behind
  earlier fan-outs (first dest p50 20–96 µs).
- The receiver measured p50 ~56 / p99 ~98 µs for the last copy, including
  the wire, where the probe says p50 ~82 / p99 ~242 µs to leave the box.
  **Hypothesis (stronger now):** the probe's own send-side capture adds cost
  to every copy on the sending CPU and so inflates the queueing; the receiver
  is the reference for kernel fan-out latency, the probe for delivery and
  ordering.

## Correction: the receiver measurement undercounts kernel fan-out queueing

- kfan stamps a shred when its program **starts** on it (tc ingress). The
  kernel hands received packets over in batches; within a burst, a shred
  first waits for the fan-out of the shreds ahead of it (~28 µs each:
  15 copies × ~1.9 µs). That wait is before the stamp, so the receiver did not
  see it. The probe timestamps the batch on entry and does count it (its
  load table: first copy p50 ~3 µs with no recent arrivals, 50–96 µs under
  load).
- So the **probe (p50 82, p99 242 µs to leave the box) is the better kernel
  fan-out number**; the receiver's p50 ~56 / p99 ~98 µs is send time + wire
  without the queueing. The tcpdump-overhead hypothesis above is at most a
  secondary effect (to be measured with `kfan.sh cost` with/without a
  capture).
- The AF_XDP comparison was biased toward kfan (AF_XDP stamped at XDP,
  before its own queueing). The tail conclusion holds (kfan p99 ~242 µs +
  wire vs AF_XDP 0.3–1.2 ms at the receiver); at p50 the two are close
  (kfan ~82 µs + wire vs AF_XDP busy-poll 66–91 µs).

## Probe overhead measured (`kfan.sh cost`, 20 s each)

- Kernel CPU per packet (all packets, ~3 of 4 are cheap duplicate drops):
  4.6 µs without capture vs 6.6 µs during a probe capture (+44%); 20% vs
  31% of one core. Per shred (15 copies) ≈ 16.5 vs 24.6 µs, i.e. ~1.1 vs
  ~1.6 µs per copy. **Established:** the probe's capture slows the fan-out
  ~1.5× while it runs, so its latencies are overstated; the receiver's are
  understated (no queueing). True values lie between (p50 56–82 µs,
  p99 ~100–242 µs); exact figure would need an earliest-point (XDP) stamp.

## Incident: local destination 127.0.0.1:20200 received nothing under kernel fan-out

- **What happened:** while `shred-kfan` was running (from 11:13 UTC, and in
  every earlier kfan test window), the service on 127.0.0.1:20200 received no
  shreds. Network destinations were unaffected. Mitigated by stopping the
  service (proxies forward again).
- **Cause (reproduced on veth, kernel drop reasons traced):** kfan hands the
  local copy to `lo` as a raw packet with no route attached; the kernel's
  receive checks then drop it: `IP_LOCALNET` (127.x destination), and with
  that allowed, `IP_LOCAL_SOURCE` (local source address). Normal local sends
  carry a route and skip these checks.
- **Why it was missed:** the probe captures on `lo` and counted the copies as
  delivered; a capture sees them leave, not reach a socket.
- **Fix:** kfan.sh sets `route_localnet=1` and `accept_local=1` on `lo` only
  when DESTS has a 127.x entry, and restores them on detach (verified: 5/5
  delivered to sockets on 127.0.0.1 and 0.0.0.0; `all.*` unchanged).
- **Lesson:** verify delivery at the receiving socket/service, not by capture.
